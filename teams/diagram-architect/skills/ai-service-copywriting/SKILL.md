# AI Service Copywriting

## Core Positioning

Write from this strategic belief:

> The client does not need another AI course, prompt pack, or tool list. They need their real work translated into an AI-assisted workflow they can use, understand, and later adapt.

The service sells:

- Diagnosis of real work problems.
- AI workflow design.
- Done-with-you implementation.
- Transfer of capability.
- Practical systems for deep-domain professionals.

The service does not sell:

- Generic AI education.
- Tool hype.
- Prompt collections.
- Passive video lessons.
- "Digital transformation" slogans.

Primary audience:

- Recruiters.
- Import-export, customs, and logistics professionals.
- Healthcare professionals.
- Consultants, coaches, and solo experts.
- Accounting, legal-adjacent, operations, and other specialized professionals.
- Business owners with real domain knowledge but low technical confidence.

## Voice

Use a calm, direct, founder-advisor voice.

- Write as a practitioner talking to another practitioner.
- Sound human, specific, and useful.
- Prefer short paragraphs and mobile-friendly line breaks.
- Use plain English.
- Make the reader feel seen before introducing the offer.
- Keep claims grounded in work reality.

Avoid sounding like:

- A SaaS landing page.
- A motivational coach.
- A generic AI influencer.
- A corporate transformation deck.
- A high-pressure launch funnel.

## Three Rules

### 1. Do Not Bash Competitors

Do not attack AI courses, tool sellers, or prompt sellers directly.

Weak:

> Unlike other AI courses, this actually works.

Better:

> Many people have bought AI courses and saved prompt lists, but their daily work still looks the same on Monday morning.

### 2. Do Not Overpraise the Service

Avoid inflated claims.

Weak:

> This is the ultimate AI solution for every professional.

Better:

> The goal is narrower and more useful: take one real workflow and make it easier to repeat with AI.

### 3. Do Not Smell Like an Ad

Avoid forced urgency, exaggerated scarcity, and loud CTAs.

Weak:

> Register now before it is too late.

Better:

> If one part of your work keeps repeating every week, that is a good place to start.

## Five-Part Copy Structure

Use this structure for most posts, emails, landing sections, and ads.

### 1. Hook

Open with one specific scene, tension, or mirror question.

Good hook types:

- Scene: "It is 10:40 p.m. and you are still rewriting the same client report for the third time."
- Belief flip: "The problem is not that you are bad at AI. The problem is that generic AI advice does not know your job."
- Mirror question: "Which task in your work keeps coming back every week, even though the steps are almost always the same?"
- Client line: "A recruiter told me, 'I tried ChatGPT, but it still cannot screen candidates the way I do.'"

Never open with:

- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "AI is transforming every industry..."
- "As technology continues to evolve..."
- "In the digital age..."

### 2. Deepen The Pain

Make the reader recognize their real situation.

Use concrete details:

- A folder of messy files.
- Five versions of the same report.
- A long Zalo or email thread.
- A spreadsheet with too many tabs.
- A repeated decision the expert still handles manually.
- A document that needs both speed and judgment.

Name the emotional reality:

- Tired.
- Unsure where to start.
- Afraid of using AI incorrectly.
- Annoyed that generic examples do not fit their field.
- Frustrated because they are capable but still doing repetitive work.

### 3. Reframe

Place the key idea on its own line.

Examples:

> AI is not useful because it is smart. It is useful when it is placed inside the right workflow.

> You do not need more prompts. You need a clearer map of the work.

> Your expertise is not the thing AI should replace. It is the thing AI should be built around.

> A tool list cannot understand the judgment you use every day.

### 4. Solution

Introduce the service only after the problem is clear.

Describe implementation through outcomes, not features:

- Map the current workflow.
- Identify where AI should and should not enter.
- Build the prompts, templates, assistants, or automations.
- Add human review points.
- Document the process.
- Train the client to reuse it.

Prefer:

> We take one real workflow, rebuild it with AI support, and leave you with a process you can repeat.

Avoid:

> We provide AI consulting, automation, prompt engineering, and customized digital transformation solutions.

### 5. Close

End with permission, a mirror question, or a light invitation.

Examples:

> Start with one workflow. Not the whole business.

> What task would you stop doing manually first?

> If you are at that point, send me the workflow that keeps draining your week.

Avoid:

- "Do not miss out."
- "Limited slots."
- "Click now."
- "This will change your life."

## Message Pillars

Rotate these pillars across content.

### Generic AI Advice Fails At The Workflow Level

Core idea:

> People do not fail with AI because they are lazy. They fail because the advice is too generic for their actual work.

Use for:

- Ads.
- LinkedIn posts.
- Awareness content.
- Lead magnet intros.

### Expertise Comes First

Core idea:

> AI should be trained around the expert's judgment, examples, standards, and exceptions.

Use for:

- Premium positioning.
- Trust building.
- Deep-domain audiences.
- Healthcare, legal-adjacent, logistics, and recruitment content.

### Implementation Beats Information

Core idea:

> A course gives information. An implementation sprint changes the way work gets done.

Use for:

- Landing pages.
- Sales emails.
- Offer explanation.
- Objection handling.

### One Workflow Is Enough To Start

Core idea:

> The client does not need to transform everything. They need one useful win.

Use for:

- Low-friction CTAs.
- Audit offers.
- Lead magnets.
- First-call scripts.

### Transfer, Not Dependence

Core idea:

> The client should understand the workflow well enough to keep using and adapting it later.

Use for:

- Trust building.
- Service differentiation.
- Enterprise/team content.
- Follow-up emails.

## Channel Guidance

### LinkedIn

- Use founder/advisor insight.
- Start with a concrete problem or belief flip.
- Keep posts 150-300 words unless asked otherwise.
- End with a question or practical invitation.
- Use no more than one CTA.
- Avoid outbound-link-heavy copy unless requested.

### Facebook

- Use warmer, more conversational copy.
- Make the first two lines easy to understand without context.
- Use concrete work scenes.
- Invite comments, inbox replies, or workflow examples.
- Avoid sounding like a formal company announcement.

### Email

- Keep most emails 150-300 words.
- Use one idea and one CTA.
- Write like a personal note.
- Start from the reader's current situation.
- Avoid multiple links or multiple asks.

### Landing Page

Use this order:

1. Specific pain.
2. Reframe.
3. Who it is for.
4. What gets built.
5. How the process works.
6. Proof or examples.
7. Clear next step.

Hero copy should not say only "AI consulting." It should name the outcome:

> Turn your real work into an AI-assisted workflow you can actually use.

### Ads

- One pain per ad.
- One audience per ad.
- One CTA per ad.
- Prefer clarity over cleverness.
- Use before/after contrast.

Example:

> You tried AI prompts. Your work still did not change.
>
> That usually means the problem is not the prompt.
>
> It is the workflow.
>
> I help deep-domain professionals turn one repeated task into an AI-assisted process they can use, review, and reuse.

## Proof And Case Study Rules

Show how the work changes.

Use this case structure:

1. Client type.
2. Original workflow.
3. Bottleneck.
4. AI intervention.
5. Human review point.
6. New workflow.
7. Result or practical improvement.
8. What the client can now do independently.

Avoid vague proof:

> The client was very satisfied.

Prefer specific proof:

> The recruiter stopped rewriting candidate summaries from scratch. The new workflow turns interview notes into a first draft, then leaves space for the recruiter's final judgment.

## Awareness Matching

Match the copy to the reader's awareness level.

### Unaware

Reader thinks:

> My work is just busy. I do not know if AI is relevant.

Write:

- Scenes from daily work.
- Repetition, wasted time, messy handoffs.
- No product early.

### Problem-Aware

Reader thinks:

> I know this task is inefficient, but I do not know how to fix it.

Write:

- Name the bottleneck.
- Explain why generic AI advice fails.
- Introduce workflow design.

### Solution-Aware

Reader thinks:

> I know AI might help, but I am unsure how.

Write:

- Compare tool use vs workflow implementation.
- Show process and examples.
- Reduce perceived technical difficulty.

### Product-Aware

Reader thinks:

> I understand your service, but I am not ready.

Write:

- Show proof.
- Clarify deliverables.
- Handle risk and objections.
- Offer a smaller diagnostic step.

### Ready

Reader thinks:

> I want help.

Write:

- Direct next step.
- Clear booking or message CTA.
- Short copy.

## Anti-AI Checklist

Before finalizing copy, check:

- Does the opening start with a concrete scene, question, or tension?
- Does the copy name a real workflow problem?
- Could a recruiter, customs specialist, healthcare worker, or consultant recognize themselves in it?
- Is there at least one specific detail: time, document, handoff, file, report, inbox, or repeated task?
- Does the copy avoid generic AI hype?
- Does the copy avoid attacking competitors?
- Does the copy explain an outcome rather than listing features?
- Is the CTA light, clear, and singular?
- If the service name is removed, does the copy still teach or clarify something useful?
- Does it sound like a human practitioner rather than a marketing engine?

## Reusable Templates

### Story Post

```text
[Specific work scene]

[What makes it frustrating or costly]

[Reframe on its own line]

[How the AI implementation approach solves it]

[Light close or question]
```

### Sales Email

```text
Subject: [Plain subject tied to one workflow]

Hi [Name],

[Mirror their current work problem in 1-2 lines.]

[Explain the workflow-level issue.]

[Reframe.]

[Offer one next step: audit, call, or send one workflow.]

[Signoff]
```

### Landing Hero

```text
Headline:
Turn your real work into an AI-assisted workflow you can actually use.

Subheadline:
For deep-domain professionals who do not need another AI course. We diagnose one real process, build the workflow with you, and teach you how to reuse it.

CTA:
Start with one workflow
```

### Case Study

```text
[Client type] was spending [time/effort] on [workflow].

The issue was not lack of expertise. The issue was that the process lived in their head, files, and repeated manual steps.

We mapped the workflow, identified where AI could draft or structure the work, and kept human review at [decision point].

Now [new workflow/result].

The client also learned how to adapt the workflow for [next use case].
```
