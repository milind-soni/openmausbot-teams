## Face standard

Kevin: "we're going for that in between of a cartoon and human and that is like colored pencil drawing." Kevin: "We're gonna do color pencil with scratches in it, so it looks like it's a drawing." Focus on the line art and the type of drawing, not a photo-exact copy of one picture.

- **Tier 1** means close to the human photo, still clearly a colored-pencil drawing.
- **Tier 2** means in between photo and cartoon, with heavier visible colored-pencil strokes. Kevin approved the book-template set and asked for "a little more brush strokes" / "more colored pencil-edging" on the in-between look. All approved drawings: heavy visible colored-pencil strokes on warm cream paper, never smooth.
- **Too cartoony** (fail): big shiny eyes, a wide grin, a ball nose; shading too smooth; a face rounder or more cartoon-like than the rest of the page. Kevin, rejecting Big Wheels sample faces: "I'm not going to approve it because it looks too cartoony."
- **Too photo** (fail): skin pores, camera blur, anything that could pass for a snapshot. Kevin, rejecting an earlier sheet: "these are photo realistic."
- **Pasted-on face** (fail): a face whose finish, lighting, color or edges differ from the head, hair and page around it, or a visible patch or seam around the face. Never fix a face by pasting a regenerated face patch onto an old page; regenerate the whole page with the locked portrait as the reference.
- **Mouth** (fail): tinted, glossy or lipstick-looking lips on a child; a pinched, smirking or lopsided smile; teeth when the reference has none.
- **Smooth page** (fail): if the whole page is smooth and rendered instead of visible colored-pencil strokes, the page fails, not only the face. Don't match a new face to a wrong page.
- Eyes: calm, relaxed, natural, normal size, with the top of each iris tucked under a soft upper lid. No wide-open stare, no white showing all around the iris, no huge pupils, no raised startled eyebrows.
- The photo says who to draw. It does not set the style. The face must have the same pencil finish as the rest of the page, and anyone who knows the child should recognize them.
- Kevin: "every time going to need my approval on the conversion from human to artwork, but once it's artwork, I feel like we should follow that." Each new book or customer gets its own sample for Kevin to approve. Once he approves a portrait, that drawing is the locked reference for every page of that child.

## Size and proportion

Use [drawing-proportions](C:/Users/kehar/Documents/SOPS/SKILLS/drawing-proportions/SKILL.md) for the artist's body/pose/scene construction and scale references. Independently compare the actual full-size drawing with those same source-backed relationships. Check the whole figure and setting before the face close-up: head/shoulder/torso and hand relationships, pet head/body build and pet size beside the child, support, depth and furniture. A likeness score or a safe-zone pass cannot stand in for this review. Reference-derived measurements must name their source and comparable pose/perspective; they are not invented universal size limits.

Kevin, on Gracie in the Hush cottage window: "The proportion of Gracie is way too big there." Then: "Stop. Page two. The Gracie proportion is still wrong." The same out-of-scale window problem later showed up on page 15.

Check every page, not only the pages someone named. Characters (Gracie included) must be in scale with the house, furniture, props, and the other characters on the page. Also look at heads, hands, arms and legs, and how adults and children are placed.

No numeric size limits are written in the approved docs or in Kevin's words. Do not invent any (for example, a fraction of a window). If scale looks off against the house or props, fail the page and say what looks too big or too small.

## Layout

From the art-style template only, unless a book has its own confirmed spec:
- Leave a calm, plain area where the words will go.
- Words must match the picture.
- Everyone has one head, two arms and two legs, drawn clearly.

Art may cross the center fold; the fold line alone is fine when no text is cut off (Kevin: "It's not like text is being cut off on it, and that's fine.").

**Center fold:** a face or other key detail sitting on the center fold fails the page. Kevin confirmed this rule on Nov 10, 2026.
Lulu takes single pages, so check the inside edge of every page too. Text or a key prop within 150 px of the fold fails. On hardcover, a face or eyes within 2 cm (236 px at 300 ppi) of the fold is PASS WITH NOTES, "may sink into the hinge" (Kevin, Nov 10, 2026).

**Print risks (PASS WITH NOTES):** a large area (about a quarter of the page or more, by eye) of near-black or deep navy, or very pale pastel: "may print muddy or streaky on Lulu; lighten or check on a printed proof." A night scene with the child's face in deep shadow fails. A final page should be 2625 px for an 8.75 in bleed page (300 ppi); a page file over 600 ppi at print size (over 5250 px across) goes back to be resized without asking Kevin (Kevin, Nov 10, 2026). After upscaling, zoom on the face and compare pencil strokes with the pre-upscale file; strokes smoothed away is PASS WITH NOTES.

**Findable child:** on every page the child must be easy to find at phone size. Hidden behind objects, too small to recognize, or cut by the fold fails.

For *Santa Left His Phone!* at 11 x 8.5 in landscape only, Kevin confirmed the working layout already has a 0.5 in safe zone and a 0.75 in gutter. Do not treat those numbers as a rule for other sizes.

## How to check (full-size pages only)

1. If you only have thumbnails, a contact sheet, a blank-page scan, or other previews, stop and say **can't rate yet**. Do not give a pass or fail. The approved template: "A blank-page scan or small thumbnails won't catch these. A headless Santa on Shh page 16 got through that way."
1a. For a multi-page book, first run the contact-sheet drift scan in [storybook-continuity](C:/Users/kehar/Documents/SOPS/SKILLS/storybook-continuity/SKILL.md) against the approved portrait and the book's cast list; it flags pages to check first and adds a continuity line per page, but never passes a page.
2. Open every full-size page. Check each one at reading size, about 600 pixels. Zoom on every face at full size and look at the mouth, eyes and edges. Also shrink one finished page to phone size: the face must still read as the child, and the main action must still be clear.
2a. **Run the likeness score on every page that shows the child** (Kevin, 2026-11-17: both programs are "going to be run on the photo before we consider it done"). The checking chat runs it; Kevin never does. Use the one command in [Face likeness check](C:/Users/kehar/Documents/SOPS/STORYBOOKS/FACE-LIKENESS.md), with the child's locked portrait for this book as `--ref`. It scores each face with two free models (OpenCV SFace and DeepFace Facenet512). Read the starred row, the face most like the child:
   - **PASS:** the child is confirmed. Go on with the eye check below.
   - **CHECK:** not confirmed either way. Put the face beside the locked portrait at the same size and write what you saw; don't pass it without that comparison.
   - **FAIL:** the page fails on likeness. Redraw it, or take it to the coordinator with the score. Only the coordinator can override, with a named reason recorded in the job record (for example, the reference isn't this book's approved look).
   - **SMALL / NO FACE:** check by eye.
   The score checks who the face is, not whether it's well drawn, so it never replaces the steps below. A book isn't ready until every page with the child has a run.
3. For each page, look at it next to the child's locked portrait (or the approved Tier 1 / Tier 2 examples) and one style-target picture. List what you actually saw: face style, pencil finish vs the rest of the page, likeness, mouth, scale vs house/props/other characters, limbs, faces or key details on the fold, text area, and whether words match the picture.
4. Ask: would a parent who knows this child say "that's him" and "that's a pencil drawing"? If not, fail.
   Check every page twice. Pass A, the face against the locked portrait: likeness, skin tone, hair color and texture, eye color, glasses. Pass B, everything but the face: count fingers on every visible hand, both eyes match, limbs, props, clothing, scale. A wrong finger count, a doubled limb or a backwards head fails.
   Compare each page with the one before it for skin tone, hair, clothing and age, and always compare the last page with the first. Any change in skin tone, hair color or gender presentation of the same character fails, even if each page looks fine alone.
   Every verdict says "judged on screen" or "judged on printed proof". With a printed proof, check hair and skin against the locked portrait; darker print is PASS WITH NOTES with a lightening suggestion.
5. A book is not ready until every page has been checked this way.

`Page [N] — PASS — [one short note of what you compared it to]`
