# Ad Art Direction: stills and shorts people want to look at

Source: research run 2026-11-24 (three parallel researchers: still-image craft, short-video craft, books/horror narrowing) plus a teardown of three failed TFB ad rounds. Evidence grades: **E** = peer-reviewed or measured test, **P** = platform's own data (has a stake), **W** = working practitioners' opinion, **J** = my judgment. Treat everything below as a hypothesis to test, not law.

## 0. The one thing to understand

People do not look at ads. They look at **something that seems to be happening**, a **face**, a **thing that is slightly wrong**, or **a question they want answered**. An ad gets looked at when it stops looking like an ad and starts looking like one of those.

Every failed ad I have reviewed failed the same way: it tried to **explain** the product (headline + subhead + tagline + props that symbolize the theme + title card + offer). Explaining is the job of the product page. The ad's only job is to make someone want to know more.

So the director's first job is not visual. It is choosing **the one moment** worth looking at.

## 1. Find the moment before you brief anything

1. Go to the source (the book, the product, the customer). Do not invent from the category. Look for a moment that is **concrete, already happening, and holding something back**.
   - Books: a scene in the first chapters where something ordinary carries a charge. A line of dialogue mid-conflict. An object the characters themselves look at (a photo, a note, a camera still). In-world artifacts are gold: they are cheap to make, feel found rather than sold, and invite decoding (Longlegs symbols, Weapons fake news site, Blair Witch missing posters) **W**.
   - Products: the moment of use, the moment just before or after, the flaw admitted (VW "Lemon"), the proof made visible (Moldy Whopper).
2. Write the idea in **one sentence with no "and"**. If it needs "and", cut half. **W**
3. Write **what is withheld**. If nothing is withheld, there is no reason to keep looking. **W** (trailer editors: "make people want to know the answer to questions we bring up"; "only allowed to show four pieces of the puzzle")
4. Check the moment against the source's truth: no invented tropes, no spoilers past the opening situation, nothing the product does not actually do. An ad that lies wins the click and loses the review.

## 2. Rules for a still

1. **One focal point.** Squint test: blur your eyes, only one thing survives. Make it at least twice the size of the next element. Pictures grab attention regardless of size, but large subjects win the first glance. **E** (Pieters & Wedel 2004, 2007)
2. **Three main elements, max.** More elements (feature complexity) hurt attention and attitude; richer treatment of fewer elements (design complexity) helps. **E** (Pieters, Wedel & Batra 2010, secondhand)
3. **A face, if a face serves the idea.** Gaze toward the viewer holds attention on the face; gaze toward an object sends the eye there. Pick on purpose. **E** (Sajjacholapunt & Ball 2014; Palcu et al. 2017)
4. **The moment just before or just after**, never the climax itself. **W**
5. **One wrong or specific detail** a stock image would never have. **W**
6. **Negative space** of at least a third of the frame, aimed at the focal point. In horror, the empty dark area is where the viewer's mind puts the threat. **W** (Toby Oliver, cinematographer)
7. **Text: one short line, or none.** If there is a line, prefer real in-world words (dialogue, a line from the book) over marketing voice. Must read on a 2-inch-wide phone thumbnail. **P/W**
8. **Off-center.** Do not center and mirror the subject. **W**
9. **Limited palette:** one dominant, one accent. Grayscale test: does the focal point still read? **W**
10. **Offer/brand line small and last**, at the bottom edge, one line. Brand near the focal point transfers attention **E**, but the offer is not the hook.
11. **Thumbnail test** beside two real competing posts at feed size. If the focal point is not obvious in 2 seconds, redo. **J**

## 3. Rules for a short (6 to 30 s vertical)

1. **Frame 1 is already happening.** No logo, no title card, no fade-in, no series name. **P** (Meta/Toluna: core message in first 5 s, 1.7x purchase intent) **W**
2. **Second 1 makes sense with sound off.** **J**
3. **The question is posed within 3 seconds.** **P** (TikTok)
4. **Hold no image longer than ~2 seconds without a change** in motion, framing, or sound. **W**
5. **Sound is half the piece.** Open on a sound element (room tone, a door, a voice), not a music swell. Silence and a hard cut to black before the biggest beat. **W** (Trailer Park's Finkelstein; Ant Farm's Brandt: "sometimes black is more interesting than any shot"). Reels are mostly watched sound-on. **P**
6. **Withhold one thing** and never show it before the last third. **W**
7. **End on the strongest image, then stop.** If horror, end on horror. Brand/offer is a short card after, not an animated logo. **W**
8. **Native beats polished.** Lower-fidelity, person-shot, direct-to-camera, or found-footage looks outperform studio polish. **P** (Meta/Toluna)
9. **Build for the loop:** the last frame should make the first frame worth seeing again. **J**
10. **Text on screen adds what the picture cannot**, max 5 to 10 words per second. **P** (TikTok)

## 4. The AI-look: tells and fixes (for AI image and video tools)

Viewers and platforms increasingly spot AI-made creative, and it reads as "ad" and "cheap." Brief against it explicitly.

| Tell | Fix to put in the brief |
|---|---|
| Plastic, poreless skin; generically beautiful faces | Visible pores, one asymmetry, a specific age; "ordinary", never "beautiful", "flawless", "8K" |
| Golden-hour glow / teal-orange / "cinematic" grade | Name ONE motivated light source and direction: fluorescent overhead, phone screen, sodium streetlight left |
| Dead-center symmetry | Off-center subject, a real camera position (high corner, low, over-shoulder) |
| Everything detailed, everything in focus | One focal point, depth of field decided and written down |
| Fog, rain, bokeh, lens flare piled on | Pick at most one atmosphere element, and only if the source has it |
| Movie-poster composites | Shoot it like a photo someone took, or an object someone found |
| AI video: warping hands, morphing faces, readable text | 3 to 5 s clips, one action per clip, slow motion, no hands near lens, no generated text (add text in post), cut between best clips, add real sound **W** (Runway guidance) |

**Format trick:** found-footage formats (security camera, phone video, scanned print, photocopy) hide AI artifacts and read as discovered, not sold. Use them when the source supports it. **J**

**Prompt structure:** subject, setting, one light source, camera and lens (e.g. "35mm at 2 m", "high corner security camera"), framing, one imperfect specific detail. No quality adjectives. **W** (Google image-gen docs)

## 5. The brief (hand this to the art team)

### Still brief
```
PIECE: [name] | FORMAT: 4:5 1080x1350 (+ 1:1 or 16:9 crop if needed)
THE IDEA (one sentence, no "and"):
SOURCE: [book chapter/line or product fact it comes from]
WITHHELD: [what the viewer is left wanting to know]
FOCAL POINT: [the one thing, its size share of frame, its position]
ELEMENTS (max 3): 1. 2. 3.
CAMERA: [position, lens/distance, crop]
LIGHT: [one source, direction, color temperature]
PALETTE: [dominant + one accent]
THE SPECIFIC DETAIL: [the one thing stock would never have]
NEGATIVE SPACE: [where, how much]
TEXT (added in post, never generated): [exact words, font feel, position, size]
OFFER LINE (post): [exact words, bottom edge]
MUST NOT APPEAR: [canon/brand/spoiler violations, AI tells]
DELIVER: [file names, folder, sizes] + one 100px thumbnail preview
```

### Short brief
```
PIECE: [name] | FORMAT: 9:16 1080x1920 | LENGTH: [s]
THE IDEA (one sentence, no "and"):
SOURCE:
WITHHELD:
FRAME 1 (described exactly, already in motion):
BEATS (with timings):
  0.0-x.x  picture | sound | text
  ...
THE BLACK / SILENCE BEAT: [where]
LAST IMAGE + END CARD: [exact image, exact words, duration]
SOUND PLAN: [room tone, effects and where they land, voice?, music? (default none)]
LOOK: [format/texture, light, grade]
AI GENERATION NOTES: [clip lengths, one action per clip, no hands/text/crowds close-up]
MUST NOT APPEAR:
DELIVER: [files, folder] + frame strip at key timings
```

## 6. QC gate: judge the idea before the pixels

Past rounds passed technical QC (sizes, numerals, safe zones) while the ads themselves were dead or broke canon. Technical QC is last, not first. Ask in this order; a NO at any step is a FAIL:

1. **Would I stop?** Look at it at feed size, cold, for 1.5 seconds. Is there one thing that is happening, wrong, or asking a question? Or is it a poster explaining a theme?
2. **One idea?** Can I say what it is in one sentence without "and"?
3. **True to source?** Nothing invented, nothing spoiled, nothing the canon contradicts.
4. **Does it look like an ad?** Title card first, stacked headlines, symbolic prop salad, stock grade, Ken Burns stills, music swell, animated logo = yes = fail.
5. **AI tells?** Check the table in section 4 at 100% zoom: faces, hands, text, edges.
6. **Technical:** dimensions, safe zones, legibility at thumbnail, spelling, no em dashes, codec/length for video, audio heard (not assumed).
7. Report what was checked and what was not (for example: "did not listen to the audio").

## 7. Teardown: why earlier TFB rounds failed (worked example)

- **"The Hunt" still (v03):** three text blocks explaining the premise; centered heroine; a fanged vampire on the ground (canon says vampires look fully human, so this is false); rain + police strobe + red wall + fog grade all at once. A movie-poster template. Fails gates 1, 3, 4.
- **"The Table" still (v02):** a symbolic prop list (badge, journal, lamp, candles) under a list headline. Tasteful, but nothing is happening and no question is asked. Fails gate 1.
- **"Porch Light" short (v03):** opens on a series title card (ad tell), Ken Burns over stills, fog, a fanged corpse (canon break), blurb-voice captions, animated end card. Fails gates 1, 3, 4.
- **Root cause:** the brief started from the theme ("vampire noir, dread, family at stake") instead of a moment from the book. Fix: section 1 first, always.

## 8. Measuring (summary; see attention-to-sale for full method)

Organic posts are free tests. Two versions that differ in one element. Hook rate (3 s views / impressions) for shorts, thumb-stop and CTR for stills. About 2,000 impressions per variant before calling it. Keep a spend-vs-return log for any paid test.
