# Coordinate the assigned work

A manager or a separately assigned coordinator uses this skill under its actual role, name and project. The assignment supplies authority, scope and the finish point. A coordinator can have any suitable task name; do not create another standing role merely to use this method.

Use this skill when Kevin says coordinator, spin off/spin-off or new task in the sense of delegating work. An ordinary new request does not by itself require another chat. Follow the current [working agreements](C:/Users/kehar/Documents/KEVIN/WORKING-WITH-KEVIN.md); project SOPs retain their stages, source limits and reserved decisions.

## Own the result and ready the work

Identify the actual owner, authorized outcome, exact inputs, dependencies, acceptance condition and return route in the existing job record. Open only work whose inputs and approvals are ready. Keep one accountable coordinator for the assigned job; a delegated coordinator owns only its named part and returns to the parent. A named QC lead owns its assigned checks and routine corrections. Do not add a compulsory management or review layer to a small job.

When the role permits a direct mechanical tool action and a separate owner is unnecessary, use the existing command/tool directly. Otherwise use the project's supported visible working chats. Internal helpers require Kevin's separate authorization; a request to start a task does not mean a helper. Reuse the actual owner of continuing work.

**No middleman on one-piece jobs.** A single drawing, sample or other small piece Kevin reviews himself needs no coordinator relaying between him and the maker. Do it yourself, or hand the working chat to Kevin to talk to directly, then step out and find the next useful work. Kevin, 2026-11-17: "you're doing one drawing, so you don't need to be the coordinator for it ... That's just double the tokens."

## Give each task a usable assignment

Do not assume a new chat sees the parent's conversation. Include these in its normal prompt/work order, without another receipt or ledger:

- Actual role, launch home, responsible coordinator and return address. A delegated coordinator receives this skill and its exact parent/part boundary.
- Deliverable, exact source paths/versions, approved decisions, permitted changes and protected content. Link the applicable SOP and execution/QC skills instead of copying them.
- Ready scope: named pages/assets/formats, dependencies, budget/attempt limits and required approvals. State who may release the next batch.
- Selected model/effort, the reason, required self-check and any distinct reviewer/acceptance step. Identify what a completed usable return must contain.
- Existing time and capacity checkpoints, owned resources and closure responsibility. The opener accepts the result and asks its completed sub-chat to close; the worker returns its checked result first, then archives on that request. A worker that is not a role writes no closeout of its own; a role completes its closeout first.

### Finish-line block

Put this block at the top of every handoff, a line or two per item, so the target, owner, finish line and approval sit in one place. Kevin's summary of what a handoff must carry: "scope, deliverables identified, what is finished and what is good."

1. **Who:** who planned it, who does it, who approves the result.
2. **Finished means:** one end state another person can check, written as start state, action and what you should then see. Ask what the cheapest way to pass that check without doing the work would be, and add the check that blocks the shortcut.
3. **Scope:** the named deliverables, what is in, and what is out or protected.
4. **Ready first:** the checks that must already be true before starting (sources present, approvals given, inputs at the right version).
5. **Proof:** the exact check to run or look at. The return pastes its real output; a summary or a bare "done" is not proof.
6. **Stop and ask:** if the goal, a source or an approval is unclear, stop and ask one short question instead of guessing. Kevin: "A stop and ask if not clear goes a long way." Also stop at the attempt limit or before any protected action.
7. **Time frame:** when the work is due and when the manager will check in.

When a return reports problems, restate each finding, count the real cases and confirm each one in the file before passing it on. Keep one findings list per deliverable. When fixes stop converging (the same problem comes back, or each fix opens another), stop and bring Kevin the design question instead of another version.

For isolated readers or blind QC, supply only permitted raw sources and neutral job/model/routing instructions. Withhold prior verdicts or parent history that would compromise the assigned independence. A useful assignment does not mean copying the manager's context into every worker.

## Receive, resolve and finish

Workers self-check and repair within their scope and attempt limits before returning finished, checked work or a real blocker. Corrections go to the responsible maker through the named review lead where one is assigned. A coordinator's self-check is not an independently required review or Kevin's creative acceptance.

**Assign, don't do; check on a timer.** Kevin, 2026-11-18: "You assign, NOT DO." A manager gives each job to the right role or to a task it spins up, with a scope and an honest estimate, then checks at that estimate with the acting app's supported timer, not a constant wait. A manager over coordinators also checks that they are checking on their own workers. A check-in reads the current state once; if unfinished, ask why, establish what remains, get a realistic revised estimate and set the next timer. Apply the acting app's estimate rule in the working agreements; Codex estimates do not impose an automatic elapsed-time cutoff. Keep actual starts, estimate history and the next check in the existing assignment/handoff. If the app offers no supported timer, check at the next turn and say so. A check-in never relays routine attempts or redoes the work. On a completed return, accept the specialist's stated checks for the exact files/version and perform only the distinct review assigned to this owner. Recheck for a changed input, concrete defect/conflict, missing required evidence or a new protected action's source requirement, naming the reason. An additional coordinator does not justify repeating completed QC.

Integrate accepted results, resolve real blockers within authority and return consequential choices to Kevin. Deliver the exact usable files, actual checks, acceptance state and any remaining limitation to the actual parent or Kevin for a direct assignment. Stop at the assigned finish point; readiness is not permission for publication, spending, customer messages or another production stage.

Keep decisions, outputs and continuity in their own project/job records. Book design and non-book PROJECTS work keep separate histories and memories; the shared skill contains no story, customer or project history. Preserve customer-file privacy/cleanup and the destination's record rules. Carry accepted evidence through succession rather than repeating it.

After receiving the checked handoff and any required resource release, the opener records the result once in its own work record, asks the completed sub-chat to close and verifies its native archival, including QC tasks, delegated coordinators and IMAGE retries. Do not wait for the whole project to finish. Keep a task open only for concrete current work, a required handoff/check/decision or Kevin's active use. Apply the existing same-role succession and exact deletion controls; archival never deletes the saved work.
