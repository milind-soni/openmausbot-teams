# Drawing proportions

Build the body and the scene before finishing the face. A recognizable face, attractive pencil finish or safe margin does not establish believable proportions.

Use this method inside the assigned [art-making](../art-making/SKILL.md) workflow. Keep its approved identity, style, tools, attempt limits and change authority. [Art-check](C:/Users/kehar/.claude/skills/art-check/SKILL.md) remains the independent full-size review. The artist and reviewer use the same proportion references and scale plan.

## 1. Give each reference a job

Bind these from the assignment before drawing:

- **Identity:** the approved child or character portrait and its source. It establishes the face, apparent age, hair and skin tone.
- **Body and pose:** a suitable whole-body or torso/hand reference for the depicted age, build and action. A cropped head portrait alone cannot establish the body hidden below it.
- **Pet build and size:** the locked whole-animal reference, plus an approved shared scene or photograph that makes its size relative to a person or familiar object understandable.
- **Scene:** the actual room, furniture, vehicle or outdoor anchors, the intended camera height and each figure's distance from it.
- **Finish and layout:** the selected drawing treatment and the assigned canvas, text space and print-safe zones.

One reference can serve several jobs if it actually shows them. Do not substitute the face likeness score for a body reference. If a physical-scale reference is missing, the coordinator selects a suitable existing reference or a neutral age/build pose guide without recasting the character. Bring a genuine source conflict to the coordinator, not a request for Kevin to solve anatomy or drawing mathematics.

## 2. Make the scale plan before the finished picture

Put a short scale plan in the normal page prompt or continuity record. State the camera/viewpoint, support surfaces, each figure's depth, the body reference and the relationships that must hold.

Use comparable reference landmarks where they are visible: head to shoulders and torso, hand to head, pet head to its own body, and the pet or child to furniture on the same depth plane. Measurements may come from the bound reference or explicitly approved guidance. Record their source and pose. Do not invent a universal head/body ratio, dog/window percentage or arbitrary tolerance. Different ages, poses, perspective and approved stylization legitimately change the apparent relationships.

For a new or difficult child/pet/room setup, make a rough gesture and composition guide at the intended page aspect ratio. Establish the setting and figure silhouettes before facial detail. Construct enough of each whole body to explain the crop: shoulders, torso, hips, limb direction and physical support. The hidden body still has to fit behind the windowsill, table, blanket or vehicle.

Place pets as whole animals at their established build and depth. Place the head on that body. A large portrait crop is not a size instruction for the animal in a room. Keep believable contact with the floor, lap, bed or other existing support.

Use the existing first-difficult-page gate for the coordinator to accept a workable guide. Reuse its stable scale anchors on related pages. Save a necessary guide with the existing prompt/art materials, without a second ledger.

## 3. Render from the body plan

Give the image tool the current action, the approved identity reference, the checked pose/scale guide and one selected finish target, under art-making's app-specific reference route. Include only the scene's needed characters and props. The body references are used to construct the guide; do not flood the final render with unrelated pages or competing examples.

Use this compact prompt structure:

```text
Action and camera: [current page action, viewpoint and depth].
Body construction: [pose, age/build reference and support].
Scale anchors: [source-backed character/pet/furniture relationships].
Identity and finish: [approved portrait and selected pencil target].
Composition: [checked guide, text space, canvas and safe zones].
```

Keep the face attached to a plausible head, neck and body. Choose framing that accommodates the correctly proportioned figure. Preserve coherent perspective, light and pencil treatment across the whole scene. Do not enlarge a head or pet to fill an available window, or distort the torso to make a preferred face crop fit.

A guide is an instruction, not proof that the render obeyed it. The final picture must show the planned physical relationships.

## 4. Check proportions while drawing

Before detailed polish or handoff, view the actual picture at full size and normal reading size. Compare it with the bound scale references and guide:

- Does the head belong on this child's shoulders, neck and torso at the depicted age and angle?
- Do the hands and limbs fit that body and the action? Can the unseen body explain the visible crop and support?
- Does the pet retain its own head/body build and a believable size beside the child and furniture?
- Do depth, camera and contact explain apparent size differences? Does the room or vehicle still fit the people?
- Is the same character's age, build and physical scale coherent with neighboring scenes?

Check the whole scene before zooming in on an attractive face. Then use art-check for its required identity, anatomy, finish, continuity and layout checks. The drawing self-check is part of production; it does not replace the coordinator's independent inspection.

## 5. Repair the cause

If the body, animal or setting scale is wrong, return to the gesture/scale plan and regenerate the whole affected figure or scene under art-making. Never paste a regenerated face onto an old page. Preserve the approved identity and skin tone. Name the one to three actual physical corrections and their reference in the repair prompt.

Background extension or margin adjustment may preserve an already sound figure; it cannot repair a bad figure. Do not protect an unacceptable depiction merely because its face score or some other check passed. Follow the existing two-failure/reassessment rule; a new method needs the assignment's actual coordinator release, not an automatic retry budget.

## 6. Handoff and review of existing drawings

Add one short proportion finding to the existing per-page check, naming the reference and actual observed relationships. Return the actual picture and its normal evidence. Do not create a separate score, percentage accuracy claim or approval receipt.

When asked to review existing work, inspect the current selected full-size drawings and any held candidate proposed for reuse. Compare the same body/pet/scene relationships above. Retain valid identity or technical checks, but make a fresh proportion decision where this defect was missed. Mark the exact affected pages for whole-figure/scene reconstruction and hold their downstream reuse. A contact sheet can find drift; it cannot pass proportions by itself.
