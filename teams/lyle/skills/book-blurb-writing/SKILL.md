# Book Blurb Writing

Kevin's book blurb and series-description method. Only write a blurb when Kevin explicitly asks for one — never generate unsolicited blurbs automatically from edits, beta reads, or critiques. Existing approved studio blurbs live under `C:\Users\kehar\Documents\BOOKS\Blurbs\` (read-only unless Kevin gives full approval to save a new final blurb).

*Provenance: Ported and cleaned for OpenMausBot from Kevin's Grok export (2026-11-23).*

## Hard Limits

- **Book blurb:** about **90 words**, **one named character at most**, and **nothing beyond the opening situation**. Convey what the book is and feels like rather than summarizing plot events.
- **Pitch card:** core pitch at most **40 words**. Sequel pitch cards add "Since book N" and "Bigger now" (at most **20 words** each), plus earlier books' approved blurbs.
- **Truth sheet (checker only):** at most **150 words**.
- **Series description:** short and to the point — genre and feel, lead or pair by role, and what every book delivers. No names, cases, events, or twists (exception: TFB series copy names the lead per Kevin's direction).
- Kevin's explicit brief and accepted wording always win; older approved examples (such as earlier longer blurbs) do not override the current 90-word / one-name standard.
- **Zero em dashes** anywhere in blurbs or public copy.

## Split Workflow: Preparer vs. Blind Writer

1. **Preparer (book-aware):** builds one Pitch Card (40-word core pitch, genre/feel, opening-safe human pressure, and supported reader attractions — never a plot inventory) and a separate Truth Sheet (<=150 words of verified canon facts) from the current synopsis.
2. **Writer (blind):** works in a clean thread seeing ONLY the Pitch Card, Kevin's brief or kept lines, and approved studio blurb examples — never the manuscript, full synopsis, truth sheet, or reader reports. A thread that already holds manuscript or synopsis context cannot act as blind writer.

## Drafting Rules

- Study approved examples for *why* they work without borrowing phrasing or another series' story.
- Make a specific human predicament felt through selective, opening-safe detail, and sustain that promise from opening line through the ending (do not follow a sharp hook with a flat case summary).
- Questions and declarations both work. Preserve the lead's desire and causal logic, leave the answer open, and cut any line that explains an effect after it already landed.
- **Sequels:** show what changed in the returning reader's experience and what is bigger now (discovery, intimacy, or responsibility — not just a louder threat). Do not re-sell Book 1.
- Verify exact word count, em-dash count (`0`), and capitalized proper names (at most 1 character name) using Python before delivery. Run the `public-writing` pattern check.

## Truth Check (Book-Aware Checker)

**"Success is when a reader's review matches the blurb."**
- Check the draft strictly against the Truth Sheet and current canon for false or misleading claims (not style).
- Keep suspicion distinct from knowledge, a long life from a long career, unwitting involvement from deliberate wrongdoing, and one person from "everyone."
- Never invent a stronger event, species, motive, threat, relationship, countdown, or resolution. If no reader feedback exists yet, say so rather than claiming reader validation.

## Delivery & Approval Gate

- Give Kevin **one recommended draft** directly in chat in a copyable block (never bury wording choices in a PDF), with a short plain summary (5-10 lines max).
- Preserve Kevin's exact accepted opening if he approved one; liking an opening does not approve the body.
- Only Kevin's explicit full approval authorizes saving a final blurb to `C:\Users\kehar\Documents\BOOKS\Blurbs\<CODE>.md` (with his dated approval words) or touching any live listing copy. Leave approved blurbs untouched unless Kevin asks to revise them.
