## Evidence labels
Tag each claim a decision rests on:
- OFFICIAL: the platform's own page.
- DATA: published numbers, often self-reported.
- OPINION: a practitioner, often one who sells services.
- OUR RESULT: our own dated outcome from a results log.
- INFERENCE: our reasoning, not evidence.
Mark single-source claims weak. Write "unknown" for any number not seen. Never estimate sales. A past answer is not evidence for itself. Clicks are not downloads: name what was measured and who measured it.

## 1. Research first
For an assigned push or research refresh, reuse still-valid evidence and resolve the actual gaps:
- Pin the book: the reader's exact subgenre ("mafia romantic suspense", not "romance"), heat, tone, series position, price, Kindle+ status, reviews, budget, limits (AI disclosure, faceless, Select).
- Find about 10 comps from the last 3 years in the 2 best-fit Amazon lists and their also-boughts. Pick by premise and voice, not chart rank.
- Note what most share: cover, blurb hook, subtitle and series line, price, Kindle+, categories, what readers praise and pan.
- Find where those readers are, with each place's self-promo and AI rules, quoted and dated.
- List the book's gaps against the comps. Rank 3 to 5 moves by impact per dollar.
- Fix packaging before spending: series linked, title and subtitle match the cover, blurb opens on the hook, last page in place.

## 2. What drives sales
- Recent sales drive Amazon rank; free downloads don't count toward paid rank (OFFICIAL).
- Book 1 brings readers in cheaply; later books earn. Land every push while there's a next book to buy.
- Release close together. Readers drift in long gaps (OPINION); more books sooner lines up with more income (DATA).
- Breakouts can't be bought. Get a good book to its first genre readers and let them talk (DATA).

## 3. The last page
Every book ends with a one-click link to the next book, the Amazon Follow link, a newsletter sign-up and a short optional review request. Book 1 to book 2 is the biggest leak in a series (DATA, Goodreads proxy). Check the live ebook, not the manuscript.

## 4. Free days and deals
- Select gives 5 free days or one Countdown Deal per 90-day term, not both. A Countdown freezes the price 30 days before and 14 after (OFFICIAL).
- Free days build reach, read-through and Kindle+ reads, not paid rank. The effect follows download volume, which takes outside promotion (OPINION).
- Expect few reviews: about 1 rating per 150 to 700 downloads (DATA, weak).
- Start with one well-fitting deal newsletter on day 1 before any stack. Book in the book's true genre bucket, only after the free days show as Scheduled.
- Before buying, write the expected downloads and ratings and check the cost fits the cap.
- Recheck price, date, review minimum and AI rule the day you prepare a booking.

## 5. Reviews within the rules
- Free or discounted copies are fine if a review is never required or steered (OFFICIAL). No swaps, pay, family, gift cards or fake fans.
- In Select, send no ebook files. ARC services call it a gray area. Safest: ARCs before Select starts, or Amazon gift copies. Kevin decides any gray area.
- Use honest AI labels. Skip any venue that makes the author affirm no AI when the book used it.
- About 1% of free downloaders review (OPINION). Plan for small numbers.

## 6. Ads last
- Ads wait for 3+ books, some reviews and a page that converts (OPINION; break-even math in the playbook).
- Judge a target at 15 to 30 clicks. About 1,000 impressions with 0 clicks is a listing or relevance problem, not a bid problem (OPINION; OUR RESULT: NNF ad, about 2,900 impressions, 0 clicks, Nov 2026).
- ACOS leaves out Kindle+ reads. Judge by total royalty, KENP included.
- Amazon Ads belong to the marketing role (Kevin, 2026-11-15). Account changes follow that role's rule: prepare, and Kevin acts unless his words authorize one exact action. Ad changes happen only when Kevin prompts them; once he has talked the plan through, the role follows his directions without asking at each step.

## 7. Social media basics
- Point social at a dated event: a free day or a release.
- Answer real "books like X" requests as a disclosed author. Reply in reader spaces more than writer circles.
- Read each community's self-promo and AI rules the day you post. Many big book subs ban promo.
- Followers, likes, reach and clicks are signals. The goal is qualified readers, an owned audience (newsletter, Amazon Follow) and read-through.
- Renew hooks and visuals. Don't repeat the same post.
- A newsletter tells fans about the next book; it doesn't find new ones. One email per release. Its gift must sit outside Kindle+.

## 8. Judge by money, one change at a time
- Measure royalties (sales plus KENP, across the series) against cost over a stated window: 14 days for ad attribution, 30 and 60 days for promos and read-through.
- Change one thing at a time, and log it before it runs.
- Weak early numbers mean pause and look, not proof of cause.

## 9. Results log
One results-log.csv per series, one row per move:
date,book,move,site_or_channel,cost,clicks,downloads,paid_units,kenp_pages,ratings_before,ratings_7d,ratings_30d,royalties_30d,status,source,notes
- Status: proposed, approved, ran, measured or dropped. For approved, quote Kevin's words in notes.
- Write the row before the move. Fill results only after it runs, and name the source (KDP reports or the vendor).
